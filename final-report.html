Why the offers went quiet

Dispatch gave responders 30 seconds less to answer. A missed offer still carried the same ranking penalty as a decline, so a timeout could make the next offer less likely. Acceptance fell after release, and some responders’ offer counts collapsed.

Release behavior

A missed offer could cost the next one

01 · Less time

Dispatch withdrew unanswered offers at 60 seconds and moved to the next responder.

02 · Same penalty

Both a timeout and a decline subtracted 0.12 from the history score.

03 · Fewer chances to recover

History still contributed to ranking. Acceptance added 0.08. Time alone restored zero points.

The code establishes how the penalty worked. The logs contain sent offers only, so they cannot identify which unsent offers Farlight would have received. The release also raised proximity’s weight from 0.45 to 0.60 and lowered history’s from 0.40 to 0.25. The record cannot isolate the effects of weighting, the shorter window or seasonality. Acceptance reached 72.7% in the last recorded week.

RULES TO DEMONSTRATE

- Missed offers expire after 60 seconds without a score deduction.
- Explicit declines advance routing without a score deduction and clear the miss streak.
- Three qualifying consecutive misses within a rolling hour can trigger an advisory “Still available?” prompt.
- A qualifying miss requires a confirmed receipt leaving at least 30 seconds to respond.
- Missing, late or invalid receipts do not count and break the streak.
- Prompts do not pause eligibility or block routing.
- Apply the proposed cooldown and reset rules.
- Repair raises an approved, verified stored score below 0.50 to 0.50 once; higher scores stay unchanged.
- Missing or stale scores require review, not an invented default or success message.
